      Plastic type guide: where to find the code and what each code means

      The recycling number is usually molded or printed inside the chasing-arrows triangle on the bottom, side, or back of a plastic item. On bottles, tubs, clamshells, and food containers, check the underside first, then the rim, neck, or label area if you do not see it right away.

      The number identifies the resin type, not a universal recycling decision. Local programs may accept some numbered plastics and reject others. The types of plastic accepted in your area can be found by either entering your location, using your current location, or providing a direct URL to your local recycling guidelines at the top of this page. After a moment your local recycling regulations can be viewed.

      • 1 - PET or PETE: water and soda bottles, some salad dressing bottles, peanut butter jars.
      • 2 - HDPE: milk jugs, detergent bottles, shampoo bottles, household cleaner bottles.
      • 3 - PVC: clear food packaging, some pipes, vinyl items, some cling wrap. Often not accepted curbside.
      • 4 - LDPE: squeezable bottles, bread bags, frozen food bags, some shopping bags. Acceptance varies.
      • 5 - PP: yogurt tubs, prescription bottles, takeout containers, bottle caps, straws. Acceptance varies.
      • 6 - PS: foam cups, foam trays, disposable cutlery, egg cartons, some clamshells. Often not accepted curbside.
      • 7 - Other: mixed plastics, bioplastics, polycarbonate, and specialty containers. Usually needs extra checking.

      If a container has no number, it may still be recyclable locally, but you should check the material shape, label, and your town rules before placing it in the bin.
